MEASURED RESULT
Same Xperimental Shuttle and six weapons, 24 native shots. The four controls
had 56/56/56/55 position intervals and six correlated native velocity/trajectory
readbacks each. Position-derived sector speeds were 0, 100.000, 99.999 and
100.000 m/s. Native speeds were 0, 0, 100, 100 m/s. Median projectile lateral
velocities (sector X) were:
- Stationary: 1.055 m/s.
- Production pose-driven motion at 100 m/s: 1.558 m/s.
- Pose-driven motion with explicit native 100 m/s: 99.549 m/s.
- Native translation with orientation correction: 100.787 m/s.
Forward projectile velocity was approximately 2300 m/s in every case.
All four controls and 24 trajectories passed the updated validator, with no
MD/probe errors or malformed readbacks. Maximum pre-correction angle in the
settled firing window was 0.001029 rad; maximum native per-shot forward/up basis
component error was 0.00103457 (limit 0.002). Serial, phase, birth time and sample
start/end/delta time correlations passed. Normal saves were preserved and the
native game and relay closed afterward.

These observations show that pose-driven motion does not give this ship its
corresponding native velocity. Its bullets omit the lateral motion observed
with native velocity. The independent direction bounds are much smaller than
the approximately 100 m/s lateral difference. Weapon spread means these medians
are not exact trajectory equivalence. General aiming/hits remain untested.

CONTROL AND API LIMITS
The diagnostic explicitly holds orientation at zero in EVERY case, preserving
actual position. The native-translation case suppresses production network
position application, but still corrects orientation. It is not uncontrolled
flight. The synthetic pilot sends NAV2/FIRE2; a real Linux engine fires bullets.
Native MD .velocity is cluster-relative but expressed in component-local
rotational axes. Trajectories use sector-relative positions. The setter's frame
at nonzero headings and differently oriented parent spaces needs its own test.
The UI speed display is a separate value, not used as native speed acceptance.
