X4 MULTIPLAYER — HUMAN DAMAGE TEST PROCEDURE
Revision 2 — 24 October 2026 — INTERNAL

STATUS / WHEN TO USE
For the matched7.4.0 INTERNAL Windows/Linux candidate and its server. Enabled
cases and progression are assigned in NEXT-TEST.txt. Earlier7.3.1 packages have
no damage correction and must not join this session. This document launches
nothing; the two humans operate their own games and collect results.

PURPOSE
Check that two real games agree on damage to the two cooperative player ships.
This does not accept NPC combat, LAN Targets, the test freighter or station damage.
Each target type needs its own later implementation and acceptance procedure.
Do not repeat accepted cargo, radar, station-layout or mission checks here.

BEFORE THE RUN
- Both testers use the exact assigned pair; the relay comes from that pair too.
- Use only the multiplayer test profile and shared starting world. Do not load a
  normal campaign. Follow the package's restart instructions when a reset is needed.
- Copy HUMAN-TEST-RESULT.txt and fill in run ID, version, OS/roles and enabled cases.
  Example run ID: damage-20261024-01. Use one agreed clock and record its timezone;
  note a known clock difference rather than changing computer clock settings.
- Use the supplied Shuttles and their default primary weapons/group. Record any
  difference. Test in clear space, about 1 km apart and within weapon range.
  Avoid NPC fire, station traffic, collisions, repairs and other concurrent actions.
- Select the same victim ship on both screens. Record its name/code on each PC.
  Read numeric hull/shield values where available. Otherwise mark them unavailable
  and keep screenshots/video of the bars; do not invent a numeric reading.

FOR EVERY ENABLED CASE
Say or note the case ID and start time. Record BOTH views before firing, immediately
after stopping and roughly 5 seconds later, using readings OR screenshots/video.
Record hull and shield separately; do not transcribe numbers already clear in the
attached evidence or captured by the candidate's assigned diagnostic telemetry.
Shield regeneration can change later readings: note their times and any recharge;
do not compare values captured at different times as if they were simultaneous.
Record PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE / NOT RUN. A visible hit alone is not a PASS.
If values disagree, a ship unexpectedly dies, or controls/connection fail, stop
shooting and collect both diagnostics before restarting. Do not repeat the failure
several times unless the developer requests a specific follow-up.

D01 — STATIONARY, HOST SHOOTS GUEST
Both pilots stop their ships. Record starting values. Host fires one short burst
at the guest's Shuttle, then stops. Guest does not shoot, repair or change seats.
Observe damage on both screens and whether values jump back after firing ends.
Expected: both views converge within the candidate's stated timing/tolerance;
no duplicate damage or unexplained healing. Record actual observations even when
the candidate's tolerance is not yet known; an unspecified threshold is INCONCLUSIVE.

D02 — STATIONARY, GUEST SHOOTS HOST
Repeat D01 with attacker/victim roles reversed. Record the new starting values;
do not assume the previous target is fully repaired. Stop after the first mismatch.

D03 — NONLETHAL HULL DAMAGE (ONLY AFTER D01/D02 PASS)
Use the attacker/victim direction assigned by the developer. Fire short bursts
with pauses until shields are depleted, then observe a small hull decrease.
Stop promptly; preserve ample hull for a nonlethal check. Compare both views and
watch for hull jumping back. Do not interpret shields alone as hull-damage success.

D04 — MOVING VICTIM (ONLY AFTER STATIONARY CASES PASS)
The assigned victim flies straight at a low steady speed. The attacker uses the
same weapon/group and fires short bursts. Record both health views. Note visible
hits/misses separately: visual shot alignment and shared health are different checks.

D05 — DELIBERATE MISS / FREE AIM
Keep the victim selected, aim clearly beside it and fire a short burst into empty
space. Do not aim through other objects. Record both views. Expected: selection
alone causes no damage; the remote visual follows the shooter's actual direction.
Native spread and gun tracking can cause occasional misses even when aimed at a
ship. Those are not automatically synchronization failures. Never compare the
number of visible tracers with damage without accounting for actual impacts.

D06 — STOP FIRING / RECHARGE
After a small shield loss, stop firing completely and observe both screens until
recharge begins. Record the last hit and recharge times. Check for additional
remote shots, repeated damage, or replay effects delaying recharge. Recharge
parameters belong to the supplied shield; no universal delay is assumed here.

D07 — LETHAL DAMAGE / SURVIVING PLAYER (ONLY IF EXPLICITLY ENABLED)
Use the assigned target direction and disposable test ships. Continue short
bursts until destruction. Expected: the victim ejects before ship destruction,
the survivor remains controllable, and a later burst into empty space still
works. Record the ejection/destruction order on both screens. Do not shoot the
spacesuit or attempt a respawn/boarding chain in this case.

LATER CASES — NOT ENABLED BY THIS DOCUMENT
Pilot handoff during combat, reconnect, simultaneous attackers and NPC/station
damage need separate candidate-specific instructions. Use HUMAN-DOCK-TEST.txt
for the independent station occupancy/boarding/handoff checks. Local engine
checks with a synthetic peer do not count as completing these two-player cases.

COLLECT EVIDENCE ON BOTH PCS BEFORE RESTARTING
1. Run 06-DIAGNOSTICS.cmd (Windows) or 06-DIAGNOSTICS.sh (Linux) from the package.
2. Copy these current files from that package's logs/ into a new run-and-role folder:
   x4-debug.txt, x4-mod-diagnostics.txt, internal-test-info.json.
   On the HOST also include server-status.json and the server-*.jsonl belonging
   to this run. If a file is missing, note that; do not fabricate or rename another run.
3. Add the completed result sheet and any screenshots/short clips. Zip the selected
   files as RUN-ID-HOST.zip and RUN-ID-GUEST.zip. Preserve them before a later
   diagnostics call can replace the copied engine logs. Do not edit raw log lines.
4. Share privately in the development chat. Do not zip the entire game/package;
   exclude JOIN-SESSION.json, session.json and ui/config.lua. The current launcher
   redacts this session's key from collected text logs; paths/IPs can remain.

VIDEO / LIVE OBSERVATION
Video is optional. For a visual discrepancy, a short clip from each side showing
the target, HUD, a case/time marker and a few seconds before/after the event is
useful. Screenshots at the agreed points are sufficient for a first report.
Announce the test in the active development chat if local live log observation is
wanted. No unattended monitor is currently installed by this procedure. Remote
guest logs still need to be supplied unless a separate read access is arranged.
7.3.1 diagnostics are not a complete per-projectile trace; the next damage
candidate may add targeted telemetry. Existing logs alone cannot prove every hit.
